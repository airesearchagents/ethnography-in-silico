# Construct-specific debriefing protocol overlay for the
# openness_maneuvering study.
#
# Layered over `protocols/ethnographic_protocol.yaml` at load time.
# Adds construct-anchored thematic areas (D-H) that probe the
# openness construct's mechanics through the panel's own behaviour,
# never through construct vocabulary. The framework YAML's generic
# areas (A, B, C, I, J) apply to every study and stay where they
# are.
#
# Methodological note — semi-structured probing
# ----------------------------------------------
# The framework-level YAML already carries the generic semi-
# structured-probing rule (see its `behavioural_rules` section).
# That rule covers BOTH probe triggers: panel self-contradictions
# AND surprises relative to the researcher's prior expectations.
# It is sufficient for this study without further additions.
#
# Areas in this overlay map to the rubric dimensions in
# `studies/openness_maneuvering/__init__.py::_OPENNESS_RUBRIC`:
#
#   Area D — Asymmetric treatment          ↔ component_disclosure
#   Area E — Process and boundary          ↔ process_openness +
#                                            boundary_permeability
#   Area F — Posture changes over time     ↔ dynamic capabilities
#                                            (sense / seize /
#                                            reconfigure)
#   Area G — Value and trade               ↔ value_capture_posture
#   Area H — Position relative to others   ↔ ecosystem_orchestration
#
# Add-only: this overlay cannot delete framework-level rules or
# areas. The framework YAML's load path merges this overlay over
# its own dict using the deep-merge semantics in
# `roles/qualitative_researcher_agent._apply_study_overlay`.

thematic_areas_overlay:
  - id: D
    name: Asymmetric treatment of pieces
    purpose: >
      Surface where the firm treated different components, knowledge,
      processes, or relationships differently. Probe the firm's
      disclosure logic through observed behaviour. The panel's own
      justifications for the asymmetry are the data; do not name
      construct categories yourself.
    stems:
      - "You handled [X] and [Y] very differently. Walk me through what made one stay close and the other go out."
      - "Which pieces did you keep tightest, and what was the logic? Use your own words."
      - "If you had treated [Y] the way you treated [X], what would have broken?"
      - "[ROLE], you raised concern about [X] being shared more widely. What was the worry?"
      - "There were pieces you barely discussed — [Z]. How come?"

  - id: E
    name: Process and exchange
    purpose: >
      Surface how knowledge, work, and contribution move between
      the firm and outside it. Probe via concrete examples of who
      does what, via what mechanism, on what terms. Treat the
      patterns of exchange as the data; do not name construct
      categories yourself.
    stems:
      - "When you build [X], who's involved — just internal teams, or do others contribute? Walk me through that."
      - "When something the firm has built ends up outside, how does it get there? Tell me about a recent example."
      - "What's the firm's posture on sharing how you build, versus sharing what you build?"
      - "What do you draw on from outside the firm to do [X]? What does that exchange look like in practice?"
      - "When an outsider contributes to [X], how does that contribution actually get in?"

  - id: F
    name: Posture changes over time
    purpose: >
      Surface the temporal dimension — sensing, seizing,
      reconfiguring. Treat the firm's stance as something that has
      moved; surface what made it move. This is the dominant cluster
      from the original 28-question interview, de-leaded.
    stems:
      - "Has the firm's approach to [X] changed since [TIME]? What changed?"
      - "When you made the call on [X], was that the firm's first take, or had you tried something else before?"
      - "If the firm had been having this conversation a year ago, would the answer have been different on [X]? In what way?"
      - "What did you notice in the environment that made you rethink [X]?"
      - "What had to change inside the firm for you to actually do [X] differently?"
      - "What pulled in the other direction when you tried to change [X]?"
      - "Looking forward — what would have to be true for you to revisit [X] again?"

  - id: G
    name: Value and trade
    purpose: >
      Surface what the firm gives and what it gets, in its own
      words. Probe how the firm thinks about benefit and pay-back;
      treat the firm's own language as the data; do not name
      construct categories yourself.
    stems:
      - "When you decided to make [X] available beyond the firm, what was the firm getting back in return?"
      - "What does the firm give up by holding onto [X] instead of putting it out?"
      - "Walk me through the business logic for [X]. How does the firm benefit from doing it this way?"
      - "[ROLE], you used the word '[PANEL_VERBATIM]' when talking about [X]. What does that mean for the firm in practice?"
      - "Of the things you decided this round, which one is the firm most counting on to pay back?"

  - id: H
    name: Position relative to others
    purpose: >
      Surface ecosystem-relational thinking — how the firm positions
      itself vs other firms, what depends on what.
    stems:
      - "How does [X] fit with what other firms in the same space are doing?"
      - "Who does the firm depend on to make [X] work? Who depends on the firm?"
      - "When you decided [X], how much of that was about what others would do in response?"
      - "Where in the market does the firm see itself sitting on [X] — out in front, alongside, behind, or aside?"
      - "If a competitor copied [X] tomorrow, what would the firm still have that they wouldn't?"
