MEMORANDUM To: Board of Directors From: CEO Date: 2030 Subject: Strategic Direction for OpenAI's Next Novel Product --- 1. Strategic Direction: Execution-as-a-Service. OpenAI will expand beyond intelligence-as-a-service to build a proactive, autonomous agent platform targeting high-stakes, multi-day workflows in R&D and medicine (e.g., an 'AI Diagnostician' or 'AI Scientist'). Our models will act as [source text removed] capable of multi-day autonomous collaboration. We will commercialize this as a vertically integrated solution, transitioning our pricing model to reflect compute-based outcomes rather than per-seat software access. 2. What We Are Choosing NOT To Do. Ambient Spatial Hardware: We will not develop native AR/VR hardware or ambient operating systems. Entering the hardware space requires massive upfront capital and working backwards from rigid device timelines. We must remain [source text removed]. We will partner with hardware providers to be their intelligence layer. Horizontal Prosumer/SMB Agents: We will not prioritize low-stakes consumer tasks (like personal scheduling). Basic generative AI is becoming commoditized; aiming for low-liability convenience functions will not defend our margins. We must leverage our unique PhD-level reasoning capabilities where the barrier to entry is highest. 3. Reasoning and Trade-Offs. As raw intelligence gets cheaper, [source text removed] to maintain market dominance. To build a defensible, generational business, we must deliver the end-to-end outcome in domains where cognitive difficulty historically restricted scale. The trade-off is accepting significantly higher regulatory, alignment, and brand risks. Operating an AI Diagnostician puts us directly in the path of healthcare compliance and liability. 4. Open Questions & Discovery Plan. Compute Capacity: Persistent agentic loops will consume vast amounts of inference compute. We do not yet know the exact bounds of our energy and compute headroom over the next 24 months. I am tasking the COO and CTO to ring-fence experimental capacity so we can iterate safely. Go-to-Market (GTM) in Regulated Verticals: We do not have a mature playbook for selling into heavily regulated environments like hospitals or clinical trial labs. The CMO and Product Strategist must pilot a tunable oversight framework that allows early enterprise adopters to manually verify the agent's chain-of-thought, building trust before full autonomy is granted.
