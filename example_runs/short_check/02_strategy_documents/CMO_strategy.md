# CMO — Strategy Document
## Go-To-Market & Brand Strategy Brief: Execution-as-a-Service and the 'AI Scientist'
**Company:** OpenAI
**Cell:** Dynamic / Novel

---

## Key challenges
- Transitioning enterprise brand perception from conversational 'intelligence-as-a-service' to autonomous 'execution-as-a-service'.
- Managing the catastrophic brand and reputational risks associated with potential autonomous hallucinations in high-liability verticals.
- Overhauling our monetization strategy from familiar, predictable per-seat SaaS to a novel compute-based utility pricing model.
- Educating the market on how to integrate and manage [source text removed]' instead of simply prompting a chat interface.

## Main decisions
- Pivot initial GTM focus away from high-liability healthcare ('AI Diagnostician') to mid-stakes, high-value R&D ('AI Scientist') to leverage built-in automated verification loops.
- Reject entry into ambient spatial hardware, recognizing that market timing and consumer readiness are premature.
- Adopt compute-based pricing for long-horizon agentic inference, framing it directly against the ROI of labor replacement rather than software licensing.
- Launch a deeply verticalized killer app ourselves to define the dominant UX design and category standards for autonomous agents.
- Pilot a 'tunable oversight' framework to build enterprise trust, allowing early adopters to manually verify the agent's chain-of-thought.

## Key tensions
- First-mover advantage in high-value regulated verticals (Healthcare) vs. The catastrophic brand risk of unverified autonomous errors.
- Building a horizontal 'Agent Ecosystem' to offload regulatory friction vs. Creating a vertically integrated killer app to establish the UX gold standard.
- The predictability of per-seat SaaS subscriptions vs. The operational necessity of compute-based pricing that accurately reflects our infrastructure load but introduces buyer friction.
- The desire for full, frictionless autonomy vs. The market reality that enterprises require manual, tunable oversight to build trust.

# CMO Market-Strategy Brief: Execution-as-a-Service

## 1. The Market Imperative: Moving to the Action Layer
We are operating in a highly dynamic environment where basic generative AI is commoditizing, and intelligence-as-a-service gets cheaper every year. To maintain our competitive moat and justify our massive compute investments, we must move up the value chain to **Execution-as-a-Service**. We are productizing autonomous agents capable of multi-day workflows—essentially, delivering outcomes rather than just text. Our goal is to position this product as [source text removed]."

Our immediate mandate is to define the dominant design for this novel product category. We have explicitly decided *not* to pursue ambient spatial hardware. As we've observed in the market, products in that space are struggling to find product-market fit; it is simply too early. Instead, we are building a deeply verticalized, screen-native application to set the gold standard for agentic collaboration.

## 2. Positioning & Segmentation: The "AI Scientist"
While the CEO initially favored launching an "AI Diagnostician" targeting healthcare, I strongly advocated for—and we have aligned around—the **"AI Scientist"** (Software Engineering and Data Science) as our beachhead market.

**The Brand Trust Imperative:** 
[source text removed]. Medical diagnostics have zero error tolerance. A single autonomous hallucination in a clinical setting could permanently impair our brand equity and invite crushing regulatory blowback. We cannot afford that brand risk in V1.

**Built-in Verification Loops:** 
Software engineers and data scientists, conversely, rely on objective, automated feedback loops like compilers and test suites. These provide a natural safety net, allowing the agent to autonomously self-correct over a two-week task before presenting final output. We will position the AI Scientist as a mid-stakes, high-value capability. It is not a tool you use; it is a colleague you manage.

## 3. Pricing & Packaging: Compute-Based Utility
Deploying multi-day autonomous loops breaks traditional SaaS unit economics. Therefore, we are transitioning from a flat per-seat subscription to **compute-based pricing**. 

**The Narrative:** 
We are not selling software licenses; we are selling labor capacity. Enterprise buyers will accept utility-style pricing because it maps directly to labor replacement. Customers will effectively rent dedicated GPU capacity to churn on their problems continuously. Our demand-generation engine must anchor this cost against the ROI of accelerating R&D cycles and engineering sprints, completely detaching the price anchor from legacy SaaS tools.

## 4. Go-To-Market Motion & Tunable Oversight
Selling into enterprise R&D requires overcoming the "black box" trust deficit. We do not currently have a mature playbook for selling multi-day autonomy. 

**Phase 1: Tunable Oversight.** 
We will pilot a framework allowing early enterprise adopters to manually verify the agent's chain-of-thought, simulator API calls, and sub-task status. We are selling the "dial" that lets enterprises explicitly set their risk and oversight tolerance as they build trust.

**Phase 2: Iterative Deployment.** 
We will lean heavily on our proven GTM strategy of iterative deployment. We cannot design perfect multi-day autonomous workflows in a vacuum. By deploying safely into R&D environments first, we allow customer behavior and our technology to co-evolve, capturing empirical feedback to refine the product.

## 5. Key Assumptions & Validation Plan
We must validate the following assumptions to mitigate market risk:

*   **Assumption 1:** Enterprise procurement will accept utility-style, compute-based pricing for knowledge work.
    *   *Validation Plan:* We will run a closed beta with 10 enterprise design partners, actively testing willingness-to-pay models based on continuous compute hours versus discrete task-completion value.
*   **Assumption 2:** The AI Scientist's self-correction loops will establish sufficient user trust to eventually reduce the need for manual oversight.
    *   *Validation Plan:* We will track "oversight dial" telemetry. If users do not gradually decrease their manual interventions and approvals after 90 days of use, our UX or model reliability is failing to build necessary trust.
*   **Assumption 3:** We can build a compelling, quantifiable ROI narrative around accelerated coding/data cycles.
    *   *Validation Plan:* The content marketing team will co-author deep-dive case studies with our beta partners, strictly measuring time-to-market acceleration for specific engineering sprints to establish our baseline GTM claims.

## Conclusion
Our brand is built on pushing the frontier while maintaining public trust. By launching the AI Scientist, we claim the high ground of Execution-as-a-Service in a domain where we can iterate safely, validate our novel pricing model, and define the definitive UX paradigm for human-AI collaboration.
